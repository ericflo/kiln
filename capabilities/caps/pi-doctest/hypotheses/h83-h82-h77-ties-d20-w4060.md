# H83 H82/H77 TIES d20 w40/60

## Hypothesis

H82 showed that broad post-read semantic edit-choice data can move the
hard-tail in one sample, but it was unstable and latency-costly as a standalone
adapter. H77 was the best recent successful-workflow regularizer, though it
also failed confirmation. H83 tested whether a cheap rank-preserving adapter
merge could keep H82's semantic edit direction while adding H77's moderate
successful-workflow bias.

The merge used TIES with density 0.2 to trim tensor-level conflicts, with H82
at weight 0.4 and H77 at weight 0.6. The lower H82 weight was chosen because
H82's verify proxy was larger than H77's, and the goal was stabilization rather
than amplifying the unstable semantic-edit update.

## Verdict

Rejected at confirmation.

H83 reproduced H82's pattern: a promising smoke win followed by a wider hard-tail
failure. The TIES blend did not stabilize H82; it introduced two zero rollouts
on the confirmation gate and made inference much slower than base. The result
also confirms that simple adapter-level mixing is not enough to turn a fragile
semantic-edit update into an efficient thinking policy.

Lesson: successful-workflow regularization has to be learned in-context with
the semantic edit signal or supplied by a higher-quality teacher distribution.
Post-hoc TIES over two rejected adapters trims the update norm, but not the
underlying instability.
