## Abstract

On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as a powerful approach for improving smaller language models from larger teachers, but its training dynamics are poorly understood. This work investigates *why* OPD succeeds or fails, identifying governing conditions and revealing a token-level mechanism. The paper establishes two critical conditions for success — **thinking-pattern consistency** between student and teacher, and the teacher providing **genuinely new knowledge** beyond same-family training — and derives practical recovery strategies for failing scenarios.

The authors show that successful OPD exhibits a distinctive signature: progressive alignment on high-probability tokens at student-visited states, with overlap concentrating 97%–99% of the combined probability mass. Failing runs display stagnant overlap and persistent entropy mismatch from the start. The paper also analyses when OPD can be expected to scale: reward quality degrades with trajectory depth, with optimal performance at 3K–7K tokens.

---

## 1. Introduction

OPD is increasingly deployed for post-training (Qwen3, MiMo, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4), but practitioners encounter cases where stronger teachers fail to improve students.

> **Key finding:** "A stronger teacher can completely fail to improve a student, even when a weaker teacher succeeds."

The paper progresses from **phenomenology** (when OPD succeeds/fails) to **mechanism** (token-level dynamics) to **practical recipe** (recovery strategies). It identifies two governing conditions:
1. **Thinking-pattern consistency** between student and teacher.
2. **New knowledge beyond same-family training** — the teacher must possess capabilities beyond what the student encountered during pre-training.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Notation

- Input prompt: $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$.
- Response: $y = (y_1, \ldots, y_m)$.
- Prefix: $y_{<t} \triangleq (y_1, \ldots, y_{t-1})$.
- Student policy: $\pi_\theta$. Teacher policy: $\pi_T$.
- Fixed dataset: $\mathcal{D} = \{(x^{(i)}, y^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^N$.

### 2.2 On-Policy Distillation

**Equation 1 — Standard OPD objective:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_x}\!\bigl[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\Vert\,\pi_T(\cdot\mid x))\bigr]
$$

**Equation 2 — Token-level decomposition:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}_x,\, \hat y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t\,\Vert\,q_t)\right]
$$

where $p_t(v) \triangleq \pi_\theta(v\mid x, \hat y_{<t})$ and $q_t(v) \triangleq \pi_T(v\mid x, \hat y_{<t})$.

**Three implementation variants:**

**Equation 3 — Sampled-token OPD:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}^{\mathrm{sample}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(\log p_t(\hat y_t) - \log q_t(\hat y_t)\bigr)\right]
$$

**Equation 4 — Full-vocabulary OPD:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}^{\mathrm{full}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t\,\Vert\,q_t)\right]
$$

Memory requirement: $O(BTM)$ for batch $B$, sequence length $T$, vocabulary $M$.

**Equation 5 — Top-k OPD:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}^{\mathrm{top\text{-}k}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar p_t^{(S_t)}\,\Vert\,\bar q_t^{(S_t)})\right]
$$

with $S_t = \mathrm{TopK}(p_t, k)$ and renormalised distributions:

$$
\bar p_t^{(S_t)}(v) = \frac{p_t(v)\,\mathbf{1}[v\in S_t]}{\sum_{u\in S_t} p_t(u)},\qquad
\bar q_t^{(S_t)}(v) = \frac{q_t(v)\,\mathbf{1}[v\in S_t]}{\sum_{u\in S_t} q_t(u)}.
$$

### 2.3 Dynamic Metrics

**Equation 6 — Overlap ratio:**

$$
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{overlap}} \triangleq \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[\frac{\lvert S_t^{(p)} \cap S_t^{(q)}\rvert}{k}\right]
$$

— measures the proportion of shared high-probability tokens between student and teacher.

**Equation 7 — Overlap-token advantage:**

$$
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{adv}} \triangleq \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[\frac{1}{\lvert S_t^{(p)}\cap S_t^{(q)}\rvert}\sum_{v\in \text{overlap}} A_t(v)\right]
$$

where $A_t(v) \triangleq \bar p_t(v)(\log \bar q_t(v) - \log \bar p_t(v))$.

**Equation 8 — Entropy gap:**

$$
\Delta H_t = \lvert H(q_t) - H(p_t)\rvert
$$

## 3. Phenomenology of On-Policy Distillation

### 3.1 Thinking-Pattern Consistency

**Setup:**
- Student: Qwen3-1.7B-Base.
- Teachers: Qwen3-4B (Non-thinking) vs Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO.
- Dataset: DAPO-Math-17K (17,000 math problems).
- Evaluation: AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AMC 2023.
- Sampling: 16 solutions per problem; temperature 0.7; top-p 0.95.
- Maximum response length: 31,744 tokens.

**Result:** Despite comparable benchmark scores, the GRPO teacher with higher *initial* overlap ratio outperforms the Non-thinking teacher consistently. Early-stage thinking-pattern mismatch cannot be recovered later in training.

### 3.2 New Knowledge, Not Just Scale

**DeepSeek-family setup:**
- Student: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B.
- Teachers: R1-Distill-7B vs Skywork-OR1-Math-7B (RL-trained).

**Qwen-family setup:**
- Student: Qwen3-1.7B (Non-thinking).
- Teachers: Qwen3-4B (Non-thinking) vs Qwen3-4B-Non-Thinking-RL-Math (trained on a 57K DeepMath subset).

**Metric — Gap Recovery Rate:**

$$
\mathrm{GapRecovery} = \frac{\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{after OPD}} - \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{before OPD}}}{\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{teacher}} - \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{before OPD}}}
$$

**Finding:** RL post-trained teachers produce substantially larger gap recovery rates than same-pipeline teachers despite comparable thinking patterns. Stronger same-family teachers add little because students were trained on identical data with identical recipes.

### 3.3 Validation via Reverse Distillation

**Setup:**
- Student: JustRL-1.5B (RL-trained from R1-Distill-1.5B).
- Teachers: R1-Distill-1.5B (pre-RL checkpoint) vs R1-Distill-7B.

**Critical result:** Both teachers drive the student to approximately the same regressed level despite R1-Distill-7B scoring higher.

> "OPD training dynamics are governed by thinking pattern rather than benchmark performance."

## 4. Mechanism of On-Policy Distillation

### 4.1 Progressive Alignment of High-Probability Tokens

**Setup:**
- Student: R1-Distill-1.5B.
- Teachers: JustRL-1.5B (successful) vs R1-Distill-7B (failing).
- Dataset: DAPO-Math-17K.
- Monitored metrics: overlap ratio, overlap-token advantage, entropy gap.

**Finding (successful run):**
- Steady overlap-ratio increase: 72% → 91%.
- Narrowing entropy gap.
- Improving overlap-token advantage.
- Overlap tokens carry **97%–99%** of the probability mass.

**Finding (failing run):**
All three metrics stagnate from the outset.

### 4.2 Optimising Shared Tokens Alone Suffices

**Three variants compared (default $k=16$):**
1. **Student Top-k:** Optimise on full student top-k support $S_t^{(p)}$.
2. **Overlap Top-k:** Restrict to intersection $S_t^{(p)} \cap S_t^{(q)}$.
3. **Non-Overlap Top-k:** Restrict to symmetric difference $S_t^{(p)} \triangle S_t^{(q)}$.

**Result:** Overlap Top-k matches Student Top-k performance; Non-Overlap Top-k is substantially weaker.

**Self-reinforcing dynamic:** The overlap region grows from initialisation (72% → 91%) due to optimisation, creating a virtuous cycle that sustains alignment.

## 5. Practical Recipe

### 5.1 Off-Policy Cold Start

**Setup:**
- Student: Qwen3-1.7B-Base.
- Teacher: Qwen3-4B (Non-thinking).
- SFT dataset: 200K teacher rollouts on math subset of OpenThoughts3-1.2M.
- OPD dataset: ~30K remaining prompts after deduplication.

**Pipeline:**
1. SFT student on teacher rollouts (Qwen3-1.7B-SFT).
2. Continue with standard OPD from the SFT checkpoint.

**Result:** SFT initialisation yields consistently better validation performance and more stable training dynamics than direct OPD. The cold-start narrows the thinking-pattern gap, enabling higher initial overlap and stronger final performance.

### 5.2 Leveraging Teacher Post-Training Prompts

**Template alignment experiment:**
- Student: R1-Distill-1.5B; Teacher: JustRL-1.5B; Dataset: DAPO-Math-17K.
- Original DAPO template: *"Solve the following math problem step by step. The last line of your response should be of the form Answer: $Answer"*.
- Teacher-aligned template: *"{Question} Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \\boxed{}."*

**Result:** Teacher-aligned template yields higher accuracy and faster overlap-ratio growth.

**Content alignment experiment:**
- Student: Qwen3-1.7B-Base; Teacher: Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO.
- Compared: DAPO-Math-17K vs deduplicated DeepMath subset.

**Result:** Teacher-aligned prompts sharpen alignment on high-probability tokens but substantially lower student entropy. Solution: mix teacher-aligned prompts with out-of-distribution prompts to preserve entropy.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Reward Quality Degrades with Trajectory Depth

**Response-length experiment:**
- Student: R1-Distill-1.5B; Teacher: JustRL-1.5B.
- Maximum response lengths tested: 0.5K, 1K, 3K, 7K, 10K, 15K.
- Training: 200 steps.

**Finding:** Moderate lengths (3K–7K tokens) yield strongest results. Longer responses (10K–15K) show *late-stage collapse*: sharp overlap-ratio drops and entropy spikes.

**Position analysis:** In the 15K setting, high entropy first appears at the *end* of the response and propagates backward through training steps (suffix-to-prefix pattern).

**Teacher-continuation analysis:**
- Sample 2K prompts; generate full student rollouts > 16K tokens.
- Test teacher accuracy advantage from truncated prefixes (1K–16K).
- Result: advantage decreases monotonically — +0.37 at 1K prefix → +0.02 at 16K prefix.

### 6.2 Globally Informative Reward Does Not Guarantee Local Exploitability

**Setup:**
- Student: R1-Distill-1.5B.
- Teachers: JustRL-1.5B (success) vs R1-Distill-7B (failure).

**Sequence-mean reward:**

$$
\bar r(y) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl[\log \pi_T(y_t\mid x, y_{<t}) - \log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid x, y_{<t})\bigr]
$$

**Finding:** Both teachers assign higher reward to correct rollouts with comparable AUROC (JustRL: 0.73; R1-Distill-7B: 0.75), yet only JustRL drives improvement.

**Hypothesis:** The failing teacher produces per-token advantages that are **anisotropic** across positions, causing gradient cancellation despite large individual rewards.

### 6.3 Sampled-Token Reward Is Already Sufficient

**Setup:** Top-k OPD with $k \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$ vs sampled-token OPD.

**Result:** Sampled-token OPD achieves performance comparable to Top-k variants. Top-1 consistently underperforms due to unstable mode-concentrated selection.

## 7. Related Work

### Knowledge Distillation
Hinton et al. (2015) introduced classical KD. Kim & Rush (2016) extended it to sequence-level distillation for sequence models. Bengio et al. (2015) characterised the exposure-bias problem of off-policy training.

### On-Policy Distillation
- **MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2023):** Formalised OPD under reverse KL.
- **GKD (Agarwal et al., 2024):** Unified on/off-policy framework.
- **Industry adoption:** Qwen3, MiMo, GLM-5.
- **Self-distillation extensions:** Hübotter et al., Zhao et al., Shenfeld et al. (2026).

### Capacity Gap and Distillability
- **Cho & Hariharan (2019):** Capacity gaps can degrade distillation.
- **Busbridge et al. (2025):** Distillation scaling laws with U-shaped regime.
- **Li et al. (2025):** "Learnability gap" in learning from strong reasoners.

## 8. Conclusion

The paper offers a phenomenology-mechanism-recipe framework for understanding OPD:

1. **Phenomenology.** Two conditions govern success: thinking-pattern consistency and the teacher possessing genuinely new knowledge. A stronger teacher can fail; a weaker one can succeed.
2. **Mechanism.** Successful OPD progressively aligns high-probability shared tokens; the overlap region grows from ~72% to ~91% and concentrates 97%–99% of the probability mass. Optimising only overlap tokens recovers full performance.
3. **Recipe.** Off-policy cold start narrows the thinking-pattern gap; teacher-aligned prompts sharpen alignment but require entropy-preserving mixing.

**Limitations.**
- Trajectory-depth ceiling: optimal at 3K–7K tokens; degrades beyond 10K.
- Instability propagation: at long horizons, instability originates at later tokens and propagates backward.
- Local-exploitability gap: even with globally informative reward, anisotropic per-token advantages can cancel gradients.
- Experimental scope: all studies focus on mathematical reasoning (AIME/AMC); generalisation to code or open-ended tasks is unknown.

**Methodological contributions.** The diagnostic metrics — overlap ratio, overlap-token advantage, and entropy gap — enable systematic comparison of successful vs failing distillation dynamics.
