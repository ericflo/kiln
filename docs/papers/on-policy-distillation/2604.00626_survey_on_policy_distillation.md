## Abstract

The paper addresses a structural weakness in static knowledge distillation: small errors tend to accumulate into trajectories the student has rarely been trained to recover from. The exposure bias scales roughly with the square of sequence length. On-Policy Distillation (OPD) reformulates training by having "the teacher provide feedback on what the student *actually produces*," reducing compounding error from O(εT²) to O(εT).

The survey formalizes OPD as f-divergence minimization over student-sampled trajectories and organizes the field across three design axes: optimization targets, signal sources, and training stabilization techniques. It unifies treatment of OPD across the knowledge distillation, RLHF, and imitation learning communities; analyzes success conditions, failure modes, and connections to KL-constrained RL; and discusses open problems including scaling laws and agentic distillation.

## 1. Introduction

Knowledge distillation (KD) has long been used to compress large language models (LLMs) into deployable students. Conventional off-policy distillation trains students on teacher-generated prefixes, creating a distribution mismatch at inference when students must generate their own sequences. The result is **exposure bias** — small errors that compound quadratically with sequence length, particularly damaging for reasoning tasks.

**On-Policy Distillation (OPD)** addresses this by training the student under its *own* induced state distribution, while the teacher provides per-token guidance over those states. This survey provides:

1. A unified mathematical framework based on f-divergence minimization;
2. A design-centric taxonomy organizing methods along three axes;
3. A theoretical account of failure modes and recovery strategies;
4. A discussion of industrial deployments and open problems.

## 2. Background and Unified Formulation

### 2.1 Classical Knowledge Distillation

The original KD formulation (Hinton et al., 2015) uses a temperature-softened distribution.

**Equation 2 — Temperature-softened teacher distribution:**

$$
p_T^{(\tau)}(y\mid x) = \frac{\exp(z_y/\tau)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/\tau)}
$$

where $z_y$ are teacher logits and $\tau > 1$ is temperature.

**Equation 3 — Gradient with respect to student logits:**

$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}}{\partial z_i^S} = \frac{1}{\tau}(p_i^S - p_i^T)
$$

**Equation 4 — Token-Level KD Loss:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Token\text{-}KD}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,y \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(p_T(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t})\,\Vert\, p_\theta(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t})\bigr)\right]
$$

**Equation 5 — Sequence-Level KD (Seq-KD):**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Seq\text{-}KD}} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_T(y\mid x) \,\Vert\, P_\theta(y\mid x)\bigr) = \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} P_T(y\mid x)\,\log\frac{P_T(y\mid x)}{P_\theta(y\mid x)}
$$

**Equation 6 — Beam-search approximation:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Seq\text{-}KD}} \approx -\log P_\theta(\hat{y}\mid x)
$$

### 2.2 Off-Policy Exposure Bias

**Equation 7 — Off-policy loss:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Off\text{-}Policy}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,y \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(p_T(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t})\,\Vert\, p_\theta(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t})\bigr)\right]
$$

**DAgger Bound (Ross et al., 2011):** Off-policy training produces O(εT²) total error over a trajectory of length T, while on-policy correction with an interactive expert reduces this to O(εT).

> **Critical remark on DAgger in LLMs:** "If the student hallucinates a severely out-of-distribution prefix, the teacher's conditional distribution may itself become poorly calibrated, because the teacher was never trained on such inputs."

### 2.3 The Unified f-Divergence Framework

**Equation 8 — Generalized On-Policy Objective:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_{\mathrm{mix}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_f\bigl(p_T(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t}),\, p_\theta(\cdot\mid x, y_{<t})\bigr)\right]
$$

where $\pi_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is a mixture policy and $D_f$ is an f-divergence.

**Equation 9 — f-Divergence Definition:**

$$
D_f(P \,\Vert\, Q) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim Q}\!\left[f\!\left(\frac{P(y)}{Q(y)}\right)\right]
$$

with $f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ convex and $f(1)=0$.

**Divergence families:**

| Divergence | Generator $f(u)$ | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Forward KL | $u\log u$ | Mode-covering (zero-avoiding) |
| Reverse KL | $-\log u$ | Mode-seeking (zero-forcing) |
| JSD | $u\log u - (u+1)\log\!\frac{u+1}{2}$ | Symmetric, bounded |
| α-divergence | $\frac{u^\alpha - 1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}$ | Interpolates Forward/Reverse KL |

#### Three Foundational Methods

1. **GKD (Agarwal et al., 2024):** Mixture $\pi_{\mathrm{mix}} = \lambda p_\theta + (1-\lambda) p_{\mathrm{data}}$. Divergence-agnostic (tests Forward KL, Reverse KL, JSD). $\lambda=1$ is purely on-policy; $\lambda=0$ is off-policy.
2. **MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2024):** Reverse KL $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta \Vert p_T)$ with mixture $\pi_{\mathrm{mix}} = (1-\alpha)p_\theta + \alpha p_T$ (α=0.2). Uses REINFORCE with $\log(p_T/p_\theta)$ as reward.
3. **DistiLLM (Ko et al., 2024):** Skewed mixture target $\tilde p = \alpha p_T + (1-\alpha)p_\theta$ and computes $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T \Vert \tilde p)$. Avoids zero-division because $\tilde p(y) \ge \alpha p_T(y) > 0$.

### 2.4 Distillation Scaling Laws

Busbridge et al. (2025) observe a **capacity gap**: when the teacher becomes too powerful, the student loss follows a power law transitioning between two regimes — initially stronger teachers improve students, but beyond critical capacity the student saturates.

**Open framework:**

$$
\mathrm{Quality} \propto N_T^{\alpha}\cdot N_S^{\beta}\cdot D^{\gamma}\cdot R^{\delta}
$$

where $N_T$ = teacher size, $N_S$ = student size, $D$ = data volume, $R$ = on-policy rollout budget. The exponents remain open problems.

### 3.3 Method Selection Considerations

**Teacher access constraints:**
- White-box logit access → DistiLLM, G-OPD, DSKD, ULD.
- API-only → OVD, GAD, LUFFY.
- No external teacher → OPSD, GATES, SD-ZERO, SSD.

**Task characteristics:**
- Reasoning/math → Mode-seeking objectives (Reverse KL, RL-augmented): MiniLLM, G-OPD, KDRL.
- Open-ended generation → Mode-covering (Forward KL, adaptive entropy-aware).
- Instruction-following → Moderate on-policy mixing (λ ≥ 0.5) with JSD: GKD.
- Multi-step agentic → Sequence-level with trajectory-aware credit assignment: SCoRe, SLMo.

**Compute budget:**
- < 500 GPU-hours → Prefix-truncation (FOPD), offline caching (Lightning-OPD).
- Medium scale → Full on-policy with adaptive divergences (ToDi, AKL).
- > 5000 GPU-hours → Multi-stage (off-policy warmup → on-policy refinement → optional RL): Qwen3.

**Stability requirements:**
- Length-inflation mitigation → Reference divergence + rollout mixing (Stable-OPD).
- Flawed-prefix trap → Token-reliability filtering (TIP, SCOPE).
- Thinking-pattern mismatch → Off-policy cold-start before on-policy transition.

## 4. Objective Functions and Optimization

### 4.1 Fixed Divergence Objectives

**GKD framework.** Mixture $\pi_{\mathrm{mix}} = \lambda p_\theta + (1-\lambda)p_{\mathrm{data}}$ with $\lambda \in [0,1]$. Tests Forward KL, Reverse KL, JSD on student trajectories. Empirical finding: $\lambda = 1$ (purely on-policy) outperforms $\lambda = 0$ (off-policy).

**DistiLLM Skewed KL (SKL) — Equation 10:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SKL}} = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_{\mathrm{mix}}}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p_T(\cdot\mid y_{<t})\,\Vert\,\alpha p_T(\cdot\mid y_{<t}) + (1-\alpha)p_\theta(\cdot\mid y_{<t})\bigr)\right]
$$

The skewed mixture $\tilde p = \alpha p_T + (1-\alpha)p_\theta$ bounds the target from below to avoid gradient explosion.

**DistiLLM-2** introduces *asymmetric divergence per data source*: Forward SKL on teacher-generated tokens (mode-covering) and Reverse SRKL on student-generated tokens (mode-seeking).

### 4.2 Adaptive Divergence Objectives

**Problem.** Fixed divergences apply uniform loss across token positions, but teacher entropy varies within a sequence: at mathematical operators it concentrates probability (mode-seeking is appropriate), while at filler words it spreads probability (mode-covering is appropriate).

- **AKL (Wu et al., 2025):** Blends Forward+Reverse KL adaptively based on head/tail-gap statistics.
- **ToDi (Jung et al., 2025):** Log-ratio routed sigmoid blending,
  $$\text{loss}_t = \sigma(\log(p_T/p_\theta))\cdot D_{\mathrm{RKL}} + (1-\sigma(\log(p_T/p_\theta)))\cdot D_{\mathrm{FKL}}.$$
- **EOPD (Jin et al., 2026a):** Continuous Forward+Reverse KL blend interpolated via smooth entropy function.
- **AOPD (Jia et al., 2026):** Asymmetric switching — policy gradient on high-advantage tokens, Forward KL elsewhere.

### 4.3 RL-Augmented Objectives

**G-OPD (Yang et al., 2026c):** Shows dense token-level OPD ≡ KL-constrained RL (Ziebart, 2010). The KL divergence to a reference policy acts as a constraint, enabling reward extrapolation beyond the teacher frontier.

- **RLKD (Xu et al., 2025b):** KD + reward model (GSRM); combines teacher logits with external verifier signal.
- **KDRL (Xu et al., 2025a):** Joint KD + RL with on-policy KL regulariser during the RL stage.
- **RLAD (Zhang et al., 2026h):** PPO-style trust-region ratio (selective teacher following).
- **AlignDistil (Zhang et al., 2025a):** Token-level RLHF via DPO + Reverse DPO contrastive reward.
- **SCoRe (Lyu et al., 2025):** Targets earliest error in trajectory (root-cause correction).
- **CMDP-KD (Zimmer et al., 2025):** Constrained-MDP perspective with hard KL budget.

## 5. Signal Source and Teacher Architecture

### 5.1 White-Box Logit Supervision

#### 5.1.1 Same-Family Distillation
Standard logit-matching: the student inherits the teacher's output distribution directly. Assumes shared vocabulary and architectural similarity (Llama-2 → Llama-3, Qwen2 → Qwen3, etc.).

#### 5.1.2 Cross-Family Distillation
**Problem:** Different tokenisers produce misaligned logit spaces.
- **DSKD (Zhang et al., 2025b):** Dual-space alignment projecting teacher and student logits into a shared probability or embedding space.
- **ULD (Boizard et al., 2025):** Wasserstein W₁ in probability space, bypassing tokeniser mismatch.
- **Delta-KD (Cao et al., 2025):** Isolates the base-to-instruct delta signal.
- **CSD (Kim et al., 2026c):** Continuous score matching, bypassing the discrete vocabulary entirely.
- **SimCT (Sun et al., 2026):** Multi-token continuation KL for cross-tokeniser supervision recovery.

### 5.2 Black-Box and API-Constrained Distillation

Signal type: generated text or scalar scores only (no logit access).
- **Lion (Jiang et al., 2023):** Verbal critique from ChatGPT as a curriculum.
- **GAD (Ye et al., 2025):** Adversarial distribution matching via discriminator-as-reward.
- **OVD (Xiong et al., 2026):** Verbal scores 0–9 (e.g. from QwQ) as outcome reward.
- **LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025):** Mixed-policy GRPO with importance weighting under DeepSeek-R1 traces.
- **ORPO-Distill (Singh et al., 2025):** Preference optimisation on teacher-ranked pairs.
- **ROPD (Fang et al., 2026a):** Rubric-based semantic scoring (10× sample efficiency).
- **DDT (Zhang et al., 2026c):** Distribution Discriminant Theory bounds on on-policy generalisation.

### 5.3 Self-Distillation

#### 5.3.1 Privileged Information (PI)
Use ground-truth or auxiliary information available during training but not at deployment.
- **OPSD:** Ground-truth answer as PI, $p(y\mid x, y_{\text{correct}})$.
- **CRISP:** Conciseness PI; condition on "generate concisely".
- **GATES:** Document as PI; gated consensus KL using retrieval context.
- **OPSDL:** Short-context PI → long-context self-distill.
- **π-Distill:** Unified PI framework with theoretical foundations.
- **OPCD:** Context-conditioned Reverse KL with experience PI.
- **OEL:** Online experiential learning post-deployment loop.
- **HDPO:** GRPO + JSD self-distill with GT-conditioning.
- **SDFT:** In-context demo teacher with Reverse KL for continual learning.
- **MSD:** English CoT as PI for multilingual safety.
- **GUI-SD:** Visual-spatial PI (Gaussian mask) for GUI grounding.
- **VISD:** Direction–magnitude decoupling for video reasoning.
- **MTP-SD:** Frozen-copy teacher (multi-token prediction) as architectural PI.

#### 5.3.2 Self-Play
Model plays against itself, generating "strong" and "weak" generations and learning from the comparison.
- **SPIN:** Self-play DPO using model from iteration $t-1$ as reference.
- **IRIS:** Interpolative Rényi self-play unifying SPIN/SPACE/SPIF.
- **SSD:** Temperature-sampled SFT minimalism.
- **UniSD:** EMA + agreement + contrastive + feature matching simultaneously.
- **RLRT:** Reversed teacher signal — flip preference order to encourage self-driven reasoning.
- **PAINT:** Partial-solution masking with overlap-adaptive re-scoring.
- **PBSD:** Preference-based reward-regularised KL with context-augmented reference.
- **RLTF:** Critique-conditioned text feedback.
- **TT-OPD:** Turn-level truncated KL for multi-turn agentic self-distill.
- **π-Play:** Multi-agent co-evolution via internally generated PI.

#### 5.3.3 External Feedback
Self-distill augmented with an automated verifier/evaluator signal.
- **SD-ZERO:** Binary correctness reward → dense self-supervision via revision preferences.
- **SDPO:** Textual feedback (outcome) + DPO with credit assignment.
- **SRPO:** Sample routing alternating GRPO and SDPO per sample.
- **CoPD:** Co-evolving bidirectional OPD: two models improve each other simultaneously.
- **OPSFT:** Generate-filter-SFT (no RL gradients).
- **RLSD:** RLVR decomposition — magnitude from self-distill, direction from RLVR (2× efficiency).

## 6. Training Dynamics and Efficiency

### 6.1 Token and Sample Weighting
- **TIP (Xu et al., 2026a):** Token importance profiling — score tokens by divergence × entropy in soft-OR quadrant; upweight high-divergence, high-entropy tokens (student confused on teacher-uncertain positions).
- **SelecTKD (Huang et al., 2025):** Mask tokens where top-1 probability < threshold.
- **AdaSwitch (Peng et al., 2025):** Binary exploration/guidance switching.
- **SCOPE (Zheng et al., 2026):** Detect flawed student prefixes early and redirect computation toward fixed rollouts.
- **AdaKD (Xie et al., 2026):** Per-token importance from gradient magnitude.

### 6.2 Curriculum and Difficulty Adaptation
- **PACED (Xu et al., 2026b):** Beta-kernel curriculum sampling problems from the frontier of student difficulty.
- **FOPD (Zhang et al., 2026a):** Prefix truncation focusing on early tokens (high compounding error).
- **TAID (Shing et al., 2025):** Temporal interpolation that progressively reveals the target.

### 6.3 Compute Optimization
- **Lightning-OPD (Wu et al., 2026a):** Offline caching of student rollouts for reuse — 4× cost reduction.
- **SKD (Xu et al., 2025c):** Speculative draft-verify amortising teacher cost.
- **NPD (Rang et al., 2026):** Async generation/training decoupling + Δ-IFD filtering — 8.1× speedup.
- **Prune-OPD (Yang et al., 2026d):** Top-k overlap drift detection; prune divergent trajectories — 37–68% time reduction.
- **R-OPD (Fu et al., 2026):** Top-p rollout + masking empirical fixes.
- **Stable-OPD (Luo et al., 2026):** Reference-rollout mixing prevents length inflation.
- **SOD (Zhong et al., 2026a):** Step-level divergence reweighting for tool-use steps.
- **TCOD (Wang et al., 2026c):** Temporal curriculum across dialogue turns.
- **Uni-OPD (Hou et al., 2026):** Dual-perspective LLM+MLLM balancing.

## 7. Understanding OPD: Theory, Failure Modes, and Cost

### 7.1 Success Conditions
The **DAgger reduction** ensures that on-policy training reduces exposure bias from O(εT²) to O(εT) when:
1. The teacher provides *interactive* feedback on the student's own states;
2. Teacher quality remains stable across the student's visitation distribution;
3. The student does not diverge into severely OOD regimes.

### 7.2 Failure Modes
- **Flawed prefix trap.** "If the student hallucinates a severely out-of-distribution prefix, the teacher's conditional distribution may itself become poorly calibrated." Unconditional token-level matching with hard-copy teacher resets causes "abrupt output collapse" (e.g., KL dropping from 2.637 to 0.343 at reset). Mitigation: adaptive trust mechanisms (TIP, SCOPE).
- **Self-play saturation.** Iterative self-play without external signal converges to local optima — SPIN improvements plateau after 3–5 iterations on benchmark tasks.
- **Diversity collapse.** Mode-seeking objectives (Reverse KL) concentrate on a single mode, discarding valid alternatives in open-ended tasks.
- **Calibration–capability gap (CaOPD).** Scaling OPD with larger teachers can increase miscalibration without proportional capability gains.
- **Thinking-pattern mismatch.** Student may adopt a reasoning style incompatible with the teacher (e.g., long verbose chains vs short implicit reasoning), producing OOD prefixes.

### 7.3 Unified Theoretical Perspectives
**f-divergence minimisation framework.** All OPD methods optimise some f-divergence over student-sampled trajectories. The choice of $f$ determines gradient geometry: Forward KL is zero-avoiding (preserve teacher modes); Reverse KL is zero-forcing (concentrate on peaks).

**KL-constrained RL equivalence.** G-OPD shows token-level OPD with KL divergence to a reference policy is equivalent to solving:

$$
\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}[R(x,y)]\quad \text{s.t.}\quad D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\Vert\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}) \le \epsilon
$$

Connecting OPD to reward-guided optimisation (RL) without requiring an explicit reward model.

### 7.4 On-Policy vs Off-Policy Decision Framework

**On-policy dominates when:**
- Long reasoning chains (exposure bias quadratic in length).
- Cross-family distillation (distributional gaps compound).
- Model scaling where capability gains outweigh computation overhead.

**Off-policy suffices when:**
- Short-sequence tasks (T < 50 tokens).
- Static, high-quality teacher data is available.
- Compute budget is < 100 GPU-hours.

## 8. Applications, Systems, and Emerging Domains

### 8.1 Industrial Deployment
- **Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025a):** On-policy logit distillation across scales (0.6B–14B from 32B–235B teachers); multi-stage SFT warmup → on-policy refinement → optional RL.
- **DeepSeek-V4:** Replaced mixed RL with pure multi-teacher OPD for consolidation; 1.6T MoE teacher → multi-scale dense students.
- **Gemma 2:** KD integrated into the *pre-training* phase, not just post-training.
- **MiMo-V2:** Domain specialists → 309B/15B MoE via multi-teacher logit + reward distillation.

### 8.2 Emerging Domains
- **Agent trajectories:** SCoRe, TCOD extend OPD to multi-step tool use (earliest-error correction; temporal curriculum).
- **Multimodal:** VOLD (vision-language), CORD (audio), GUI-SD (GUI grounding), Uni-OPD (LLM+MLLM).
- **Cross-lingual:** MSD uses English reasoning as PI for non-English alignment.
- **Long context:** OPSDL extends short-context PI to long-context distillation.
- **Code/Math agents:** LiteGUI, KAT-Coder-V2 apply OPD to agentic coding.

### 8.3 System-Level Integration
- **Pipeline ordering:** Off-policy warmup (2–3 epochs) → on-policy refinement (1–2 epochs) → optional RL exploration.
- **Multi-teacher consolidation:** DeepSeek-V4 aggregates 10+ domain experts via confidence-weighted divergence averaging.
- **Async generation–training:** NPD, Lightning-OPD decouple teacher inference and student generation from gradient updates.

### 8.4 The Distillation Tax
OPD introduces a "distillation tax" — multiple teacher inference passes per student gradient step. Optimal allocation:
- Teacher inference: 30–50% of budget.
- Student generation: 20–30%.
- Gradient computation: 20–40%.

Offline caching (Lightning-OPD) and speculative verification (SKD) reduce the tax to <1.5× pre-training cost per equivalent training step.

## 9. Open Problems and Future Directions

1. **Distillation scaling laws.** Unified prediction $\text{Quality}\propto N_T^\alpha\cdot N_S^\beta\cdot D^\gamma\cdot R^\delta$ (exponents unresolved).
2. **Uncertainty-aware feedback.** Incorporate calibration metrics into the teacher signal; methods currently treat teacher logits as ground truth.
3. **Agent-level distillation.** Extend OPD beyond single-turn generation to multi-step planning with credit assignment across hundreds of steps.
4. **RL–KD unification.** Formalise when OPD ≡ RL with explicit reward; when are external rewards necessary?
5. **Tokeniser-agnostic distillation.** Generalise beyond DSKD/ULD/CSD workarounds to principled cross-tokeniser methods.
6. **Continual OPD.** Prevent catastrophic forgetting when distilling sequential domains (code → math → reasoning).

## 10. Conclusion

### Key Findings
1. On-policy sampling reduces exposure bias from O(εT²) to O(εT), enabling longer reasoning chains.
2. Divergence choice (Forward KL vs Reverse KL vs adaptive) depends on task geometry and signal source.
3. Self-distillation via privileged information or external feedback eliminates external teacher dependence.
4. Multi-stage pipelines (off-policy warmup → on-policy → RL) balance stability and final performance.

### Practical Takeaways
- **Reasoning tasks:** Use Reverse KL or RL-augmented objectives (MiniLLM, G-OPD, KDRL).
- **Instruction-following:** Moderate on-policy mixing (GKD with λ ≥ 0.5) or adaptive divergences (ToDi, AKL).
- **Long sequences:** Pair on-policy training with curriculum and token weighting (PACED, TIP, SCOPE).
- **Compute constraints:** Offline caching (Lightning-OPD) or prefix truncation (FOPD) reduce overhead 4–8×.

### Looking Ahead
OPD is moving from a knowledge-distillation technique toward a general post-training paradigm, increasingly overlapping with RL and self-supervised learning. Industrial systems now treat OPD as co-equal to RLHF for reasoning model development.

### Limitations
- Survey covers methods through early 2026; the field is evolving rapidly.
- Reproducibility challenges due to proprietary models (Qwen, DeepSeek, GPT-5).
- Heterogeneous experimental setups make direct comparison difficult.

### Broader Impact
OPD enables resource-efficient deployment of frontier capabilities in smaller, deployable models. This democratises access but may also scale harmful capabilities if misused.
