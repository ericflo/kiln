## Abstract

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is increasingly used in LLM post-training because it can leverage a teacher to provide dense supervision on student rollouts. Standard implementations reduce distribution matching to **sampled-token log-ratios**, which creates brittle learning signals on long rollouts. The paper:

1. Clarifies a theoretical trade-off — token-level OPD is biased relative to sequence-level reverse-KL but exhibits better worst-case variance scaling with sequence length (O(T²) vs O(T⁴)).
2. Identifies three empirical failure modes of sampled-token OPD: **imbalanced one-token supervision**, **unreliable teacher guidance on student-generated prefixes**, and **tokeniser/special-token mismatch**.
3. Proposes **teacher top-K local support matching** with truncated reverse-KL, top-p rollouts, and special-token masking.

The proposed method achieves a **+19.8% performance gain** over standard sampled-token OPD baselines across reasoning and multi-task benchmarks.

---

## 1. Introduction

OPD has gained traction as a complement to RL-based post-training, particularly for reasoning models. Industrial systems (Qwen3, MiMo-V2, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4) use OPD to scale capability with limited compute.

**Standard implementations** reduce per-token distribution matching to sampled-token log-ratios. While simple and efficient, this approach is brittle in long rollouts. The paper analyses *why* this happens and proposes simple, effective fixes.

**Main contributions:**
- Theoretical clarification of the bias–variance trade-off of token-level vs sequence-level OPD.
- Empirical identification of three failure modes.
- A practical method — **teacher top-K local support matching** — combining truncated reverse-KL, top-p rollout sampling, and special-token masking.

## 2. Understanding Sampled-Token OPD: Trade-offs and Failure Modes

### 2.1 From Reverse-KL to Token-Level OPD

**Objective:**

$$
J_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim D}\!\bigl[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\Vert\,q(\cdot\mid x))\bigr]
$$

**Gradient:**

$$
\nabla_\theta J_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\!\bigl[(\log \pi_\theta(y\mid x) - \log q(y\mid x))\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)\bigr]
$$

**Equation 1 — Sequence-level estimator:**

$$
\hat g_{\mathrm{seq}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\!\left(\sum_{t'=1}^{T} r_{t'}\right) s_t
$$

**Equation 2 — Token-level estimator:**

$$
\hat g_{\mathrm{tok}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} r_t\, s_t
$$

with score $s_t = \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid c_t)$ and per-token log-ratio $r_t = \log\!\bigl(\pi_\theta(y_t\mid c_t)/q(y_t\mid c_t)\bigr)$.

**Equation 3 — Discounted return-to-go:**

$$
\hat g_\gamma = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\!\left(\sum_{t'=t}^{T} \gamma^{t'-t} r_{t'}\right) s_t,\qquad \gamma \in [0,1].
$$

**Variance analysis:**
- Token-level OPD: O(T²) worst-case variance.
- Sequence-level OPD: O(T⁴) worst-case variance.

**Toy experiment.** Two-task continuous-control environment with a 4K-parameter MLP student. Larger $\gamma$ yields higher gradient variance and less stable optimisation (Figure 1).

### 2.2 Why Sampled-Token OPD Is Brittle in Practice

**Three failure modes:**

1. **Highly imbalanced token-level signal.**
   - Most sampled tokens receive negative reward.
   - Optimisation becomes dominated by a small subset of locally positive tokens.
   - Sensitivity to high-frequency fillers and short continuations.

2. **Unreliable teacher signal on student-generated prefixes.**
   - Teacher probability on student tokens becomes unreliable on off-distribution prefixes.
   - Sharp teacher distributions amplify modest mismatches into large log-ratio values.
   - Teacher–student divergence grows along trajectories (Figure 4).

3. **Tokeniser and special-token mismatch.**
   - Student-generated tokens may not correspond to natural tokens under the teacher.
   - Different tokenisations of the same semantic content (e.g., `<think>` vs `<`, `think`, `>`).
   - Special-token mismatch distorts the reward signal.

**Experimental setup:**
- Student: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- Teacher: OpenThinker3-7B.
- Dataset: DAPO-Math-17K, max context 16K.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Teacher Top-K Local Support Matching

**Equation 4 — Full-vocabulary reverse-KL:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}}(c_t) = \sum_{v\in V} \pi_\theta(v\mid c_t)\,\log\!\frac{\pi_\theta(v\mid c_t)}{q(v\mid c_t)}
$$

**Equation 5 — Sampled-token OPD:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sample}}(c_t, y_t) = \log\!\frac{\pi_\theta(y_t\mid c_t)}{q(y_t\mid c_t)},\qquad y_t \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid c_t)
$$

**Equation 6 — Teacher support set:**

$$
S(c_{i,t}) = \mathrm{TopK}_q(c_{i,t})
$$

**Equation 7 — Renormalised distributions:**

$$
\hat\pi_\theta(v\mid c_{i,t}) = \frac{\pi_\theta(v\mid c_{i,t})}{\sum_{u\in S(c_{i,t})} \pi_\theta(u\mid c_{i,t})}, \qquad
\hat q(v\mid c_{i,t}) = \frac{q(v\mid c_{i,t})}{\sum_{u\in S(c_{i,t})} q(u\mid c_{i,t})}
$$

**Equation 8 — Local support matching objective:**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LSM}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,\,\{o_i\}\sim \pi_{\theta,\mathrm{infer}}}\!\left[
\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{G} |o_i|}
\sum_{i=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|}\sum_{v\in S(c_{i,t})}
\hat\pi_\theta(v\mid c_{i,t})\,\log\!\frac{\hat\pi_\theta(v\mid c_{i,t})}{\hat q(v\mid c_{i,t})}\right]
$$

### 3.2 Practical Stabilisation Choices

- **Support-set renormalisation.** Separate softmax over logits inside the support set; prevents gradient propagation outside the support; ensures direct comparability of probability masses.
- **Top-p rollout sampling.** Generates rollouts with nucleus sampling to keep trajectories near typical continuations and maintain reliable teacher signals.
- **Special-token masking.** Masks problematic special tokens to reduce false negatives from tokeniser incompatibilities; an orthogonal practical fix.

## 5. Conclusion

The paper:
1. Clarifies the theoretical bias–variance trade-off of token-level vs sequence-level OPD;
2. Identifies three failure modes of sampled-token OPD;
3. Proposes **teacher top-K local support matching** with truncated reverse-KL.

The method achieves a **+19.8%** performance improvement on math benchmarks while improving optimisation stability across single- and multi-task setups.

**Limitations acknowledged:**
- Teacher matching remains an imperfect proxy for task success.
- Train–inference mismatch concerns persist.
- Performance gaps to teachers remain substantial, suggesting the method addresses one component of a larger problem requiring outcome-verifiable rewards and distribution-shift handling.

## Appendix D — Bias and Variance Analysis

### D.1 Bias of Token-Level Estimator

**Expectation gap:**

$$
\mathbb{E}[\hat g_{\mathrm{seq}}] - \mathbb{E}[\hat g_{\mathrm{tok}}] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{t'=t+1}^{T} r_{t'} s_t\right]
$$

Token-level OPD removes the future-reward coupling terms, introducing bias relative to the sequence-level objective.

### D.2 Worst-Case Variance Upper Bounds

**Assumptions:** $|r_t| \le B_r$ and $\|s_t\| \le B_s$ for all $t$.

**Token-level variance:**

$$
\mathrm{Var}(\hat g_{\mathrm{tok}}) = O(T^2)
$$

**Sequence-level variance:**

$$
\mathrm{Var}(\hat g_{\mathrm{seq}}) = O(T^4)
$$

The conservative analysis captures why reward coupling becomes problematic in long-horizon training.

## Appendix E — Toy Experiment Details

**Environment:**
- Two-task one-dimensional continuous control.
- Student: 3-layer MLP (~4K parameters).
- Input: 3-dimensional (task identity, position, normalised time step).
- State transition: $s_{t+1} = s_t + \delta$, $\delta \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma)$.
- Tasks: mirror images (left: +2 → -3; right: -2 → +3).
- Training: alternating-task OPD with separate REINFORCE-trained teachers.

**Gradient-variance estimation (micro-batch approach):**

$$
\mathrm{Var}(g) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} \|g_m - \bar g\|^2,\qquad \bar g = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} g_m
$$

with $B = 64$, $M = 8$ micro-batches.

**Results:** Consistent across seeds — larger $\gamma$ yields higher persistent variance; $\gamma = 0$ (token-level) learns target-directed trajectories; $\gamma = 1.0$ exhibits drift and sub-optimal stabilisation.
