# Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning

Debashis Guha
S P Jain School of Global Management;
debashis.guha@spjain.org

###### Abstract

Every adaptive learning system must alternate between two operations:
consolidating what it already knows and expanding into new evidence. We propose
*Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics* (OpMech), a framework that
makes this structure precise. The central object is the *order-gap*
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e), the degree to which a consolidation operator QQ and an
expansion operator PeP\_{e} fail to commute at a given knowledge state. Because
the order-gap is computable from the system’s own trajectory, it serves as
a real-time control signal: large values indicate that the system is still
sensitive to the ordering of consolidation and expansion; once the order-gap
falls and stays small, further processing is unlikely to change the outcome.

Three results give the signal precise meaning: the order-gap
decays along convergent trajectories; a persistently large order-gap implies
the system is far from its settled state; and an order-gap-based
stopping rule terminates with provable guarantees in both noiseless and
bounded-noise settings. The framework applies across five domains:
bandits, reinforcement learning, stochastic optimization, continual learning,
and recursive language models. We give
conditions under which the order-gap reliably tracks convergence in three
representative cases. We develop the recursive language model application in
detail, showing how OpMech replaces heuristic stopping rules and fixed recursion
budgets with principled, evidence-driven alternatives.

Keywords.
adaptive learning; recursive reasoning; iterative refinement; non-commuting operators;
bandits; reinforcement learning; recursive language models; long context;
stochastic optimization; continual learning.

## 1 Introduction

The tension between knowledge consolidation and knowledge expansion, known as
the exploitation-exploration dilemma in reinforcement learning, is among
the oldest problems in adaptive systems. In multi-armed bandits, the learner
must weigh the arm with the highest estimated reward against trying less-known
ones. In reinforcement learning, the agent must balance policy improvement
against continued evaluation. In stochastic gradient descent, the optimiser
must choose between refining the current basin and escaping to a better one.
In continual learning, the system must protect what it has already learned
while remaining open to new tasks. In recursive language models, the reasoner
must decide between consolidating an answer from evidence already gathered
and recursing into further context.

These problems are typically studied in isolation, each with its own
formalism and analysis tools. Yet beneath all of them lies the same structure:
two operations (one that acts on current knowledge, one that incorporates
new evidence) applied in sequence, with their ordering shaping the learning
trajectory.

This paper proposes *Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics* (OpMech),
a framework that makes this shared structure explicit. The central
observation is:

> *In every adaptive learning system, consolidation and expansion are
> non-commuting operations on the learner’s knowledge state. The degree to which
> their ordering matters is a measurable, trajectory-dependent quantity, the
> order-gap; this quantity can be used as a control signal for adaptive
> algorithms.*

The framework consists of three elements:

1. 1.

   A stochastic dynamical system on a knowledge state space 𝒳\mathcal{X},
   driven by two operators: consolidation QQ and expansion PeP\_{e}, with
   canonical dynamics θt+1=Q∘Pet​(θt)\theta\_{t+1}=Q\circ P\_{e\_{t}}(\theta\_{t}).
2. 2.

   The order-gap Ω​(θ;e)=‖Q​(Pe​(θ))−Pe​(Q​(θ))‖\Omega(\theta;e)=\left\lVert Q(P\_{e}(\theta))-P\_{e}(Q(\theta))\right\rVert, which measures the effect on downstream decisions of
   reordering the two operations.
3. 3.

   An algorithmic principle: track the order-gap and use it to
   modulate the learning system’s behaviour, expand more when Ω\Omega is
   large, consolidate more when it is small.

## 2 The OpMech Framework

### 2.1 Knowledge State

###### Definition 2.1 (Knowledge state).

The *knowledge state* of an adaptive learner is a point θ∈𝒳\theta\in\mathcal{X},
where 𝒳\mathcal{X} is a state space encoding everything the learner currently knows.

The precise structure of 𝒳\mathcal{X} depends on the domain:

| Domain | Knowledge state θ\theta |
| --- | --- |
| Multi-armed bandits | Sufficient statistics {(μ^a,σ^a2,na)}a=1K\{(\hat{\mu}\_{a},\hat{\sigma}\_{a}^{2},n\_{a})\}\_{a=1}^{K} |
| Stochastic gradient descent | Model parameters + optimiser state |
| Reinforcement learning | Value function parameters θ\theta of Qθ​(s,a)Q\_{\theta}(s,a) |
| Continual learning | Task-specific parameters across all tasks seen |
| Recursive language models | Aggregated state SS over chunks processed so far |

### 2.2 Two Operators

The learner’s trajectory through 𝒳\mathcal{X} is governed by two operators.

###### Definition 2.2 (Expansion operator).

For an event e∈ℰe\in\mathcal{E}, the *expansion operator*
Pe:𝒳→𝒳P\_{e}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X} is defined by Pe​(θ)=Ue​(θ)P\_{e}(\theta)=U\_{e}(\theta),
where UeU\_{e} is the evidence update corresponding to event ee.

An *event* is whatever arrives from outside the learner: a reward
observation in bandits, a sampled minibatch in SGD, a transition in RL,
a chunk of context in recursive reasoning. The
key property is that PeP\_{e} brings information into θ\theta that was not
already there. The subscript ee matters: the family {Pe:e∈ℰ}\{P\_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E}\} is indexed by the event because a different event induces a different
update, and this distinction is what makes the expected commutator
Jacobian 𝔼e​[A​Be−Be​A]\mathbb{E}\_{e}[AB\_{e}-B\_{e}A] non-trivial, with each ee contributing a distinct
Be=D​Pe​(θ⋆)B\_{e}=DP\_{e}(\theta^{\star}).

###### Definition 2.3 (Consolidation operator).

The *consolidation operator* Q:𝒳→𝒳Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X} refines θ\theta toward the
greedy optimum using only information already present in θ\theta. No external
evidence enters.

The defining properties of QQ are:

1. 1.

   Endogenous: QQ depends only on θ\theta, not on any event ee.
2. 2.

   Contractive: QQ concentrates the state around the current
   greedy optimum, reducing uncertainty about the optimal action.
3. 3.

   Fixed-point structure: the fixed points of QQ are knowledge
   states where pure consolidation is self-consistent.

The asymmetry between the two operators is fundamental: PeP\_{e} requires
external input; QQ does not.

### 2.3 Dynamics

###### Definition 2.4 (Stochastic dynamical system).

The *OpMech dynamics* are the discrete-time Markov chain on 𝒳\mathcal{X} with
canonical trajectory

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | θt+1=Q∘Pet(θt),et∼P(⋅∣θt),\theta\_{t+1}=Q\circ P\_{e\_{t}}(\theta\_{t}),\qquad e\_{t}\sim P(\cdot\mid\theta\_{t}), |  | (1) |

and transition kernel

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | ℙ​(θt+1∈A∣θt)=∫ℰ𝟏A​(Q​(Ue​(θt)))​P​(d​e∣θt).\mathbb{P}(\theta\_{t+1}\in A\mid\theta\_{t})=\int\_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{1}\_{A}\bigl(Q(U\_{e}(\theta\_{t}))\bigr)\,P(de\mid\theta\_{t}). |  | (2) |

At each step, the learner expands (incorporates evidence from event ete\_{t}) and
then consolidates (refines toward the greedy optimum). The randomness
comes from ete\_{t}, which may depend on the current knowledge
state (as in bandits, where the arm-selection policy depends on θ\theta) or
may be independent of it (as in SGD, where the minibatch is drawn from a fixed
distribution).

### 2.4 The Order-Gap

###### Definition 2.5 (Order-gap).

The *order-gap* at state θ\theta given event ee is

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | Ω​(θ;e)=d​(Q​(Pe​(θ)),Pe​(Q​(θ))),\Omega(\theta;e)=d\bigl(Q(P\_{e}(\theta)),\;P\_{e}(Q(\theta))\bigr), |  | (3) |

where dd is a metric on 𝒳\mathcal{X}. When 𝒳\mathcal{X} is a vector space this reduces to

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Ω​(θ;e)=‖Q​(Pe​(θ))−Pe​(Q​(θ))‖.\Omega(\theta;e)=\left\lVert Q(P\_{e}(\theta))-P\_{e}(Q(\theta))\right\rVert. |  |

The order-gap measures the distance between two trajectories: one where the
learner expands before consolidating, and one where it consolidates before
expanding. When Ω\Omega is large, the ordering matters; the learning system
has not settled. When Ω\Omega is small, the ordering is nearly
irrelevant; the system has converged.

Decision-facing order-gap.
If a decision map π:𝒳→𝒵\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z} is specified and d𝒵d\_{\mathcal{Z}} is a metric on 𝒵\mathcal{Z},
the order-gap at the decision level is:

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | Ωπ​(θ;e)=d𝒵​(π​(Q​(Pe​(θ))),π​(Pe​(Q​(θ)))).\Omega^{\pi}(\theta;e)=d\_{\mathcal{Z}}\!\bigl(\pi(Q(P\_{e}(\theta))),\;\pi(P\_{e}(Q(\theta)))\bigr). |  | (4) |

When 𝒵\mathcal{Z} is a vector space, this reduces to the norm of the difference.

### 2.5 Key Properties

Non-commutativity is generically present.
In nontrivial adaptive systems, consolidation and expansion generically need
not commute: QQ contracts toward a fixed point that depends on the current
state, while PeP\_{e} moves the state, so the two operations interact
differently depending on their order. The order-gap measures the extent to
which they fail to commute along the trajectory. Non-stationarity makes the
order-gap *persistently large*, but the non-commutativity is a property
of the operator pair, not a consequence of the environment.

Convergence under stationarity (noiseless case).
In the noiseless stationary regime (σ=0\sigma=0, see Assumption [4.3](#S4.Thmdefinition3 "Assumption 4.3 (Equilibrium regularity). ‣ 4.1 Setting and Assumptions ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")):

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | 𝔼​[Ω​(θt;et)]→0as ​t→∞.\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Omega(\theta\_{t};e\_{t})\bigr]\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to\infty. |  | (5) |

In noisy stationary regimes (σ>0\sigma>0), the expected order-gap is bounded
by a noise-floor term quantified in Section [4.6](#S4.SS6 "4.6 The Noise Floor ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning").
This is not the same as [Q,Pe]=0[Q,P\_{e}]=0; the operators remain structurally
non-commutative. Only their expected trajectory impact shrinks.

Persistence under non-stationarity.
When the environment changes, 𝔼​[Ω​(θt;et)]\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta\_{t};e\_{t})] remains
large or grows. The order-gap detects that the learning system has not settled
relative to the shifting environment.

## 3 The Algorithmic Principle

The core claim of OpMech is not that the order-gap exists (it trivially does
for any pair of non-commuting maps), but that Ω\Omega is the right
quantity to *track and control* in adaptive systems.

The algorithmic principle is:

> *Compute the order-gap Ω​(θt;et)\Omega(\theta\_{t};e\_{t}) along the learning
> trajectory. Use it as a control signal: expand more when Ω\Omega is large,
> consolidate more when Ω\Omega is small.*

This principle takes three concrete forms:

Adaptive exploration rate.
Let ηt\eta\_{t} be the exploration rate at step tt. Set
ηt=ϕ​(Ω​(θt;et))\eta\_{t}=\phi(\Omega(\theta\_{t};e\_{t})), where ϕ\phi is a monotone increasing
function. When the order-gap is large, the ordering of consolidation and
expansion matters greatly: the learner has not settled, and it should expand
more. When the order-gap is small, the learner has nearly converged, so it
should consolidate.

Consolidation scheduling.
Rather than consolidating at every step, consolidate when the accumulated
order-gap since the last consolidation exceeds a threshold cc:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑i=τtΩ​(θi;ei)>c,\sum\_{i=\tau}^{t}\Omega(\theta\_{i};e\_{i})>c, |  |

where τ\tau is the last consolidation step.

Stopping criterion.
Declare convergence when a windowed average of Ω\Omega falls below a
threshold ε\varepsilon. Section [4](#S4 "4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") establishes formal
convergence and stopping guarantees for the order-gap signal. The adaptive
exploration and consolidation-scheduling rules above are algorithmic design
principles motivated by this theory; their performance guarantees are
domain-specific and developed in companion work.

## 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees

The algorithmic principle of Section [3](#S3 "3 The Algorithmic Principle ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") rests on three
claims that need formal backing: that the order-gap decays along convergent
trajectories, that a persistently large order-gap signals genuine
suboptimality, and that the stopping rule Ω<ε\Omega<\varepsilon terminates
in finite time with bounded error. This section establishes each of these.

Scope.

events ete\_{t} are drawn independently from a fixed distribution PP on
(ℰ,ℱ)(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}), independent of θt\theta\_{t}; we refer to this as the
*fixed-sampling setting*. This covers independent evidence streams and
settings where the sampling policy is held fixed. When the event distribution
depends on the current state (as in fully adaptive bandits or off-policy

*state-dependent setting* under uniform conditional moment bounds; the
contraction and stopping theorems carry over verbatim, because the
underlying concentration tool is martingale concentration on residuals

### 4.2 When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium

Assumption [4.4](#S4.Thmdefinition4 "Assumption 4.4 (Local order-gap sensitivity). ‣ 4.1 Setting and Assumptions ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") is a global, first-moment condition on the

Under σ=0\sigma=0, a first-order Taylor expansion at

###### Proposition 4.6 (Local order-gap sensitivity via Jacobian rank).

Assume σ=0\sigma=0, and that Q,Pe∈C2Q,P\_{e}\in C^{2} near θ⋆\theta^{\star} with
second derivatives uniformly bounded in that neighbourhood (or with an
integrable dominating function for the second derivatives over ee). Define
Σ¯:=𝔼e​[Σe]\bar{\Sigma}:=\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Sigma\_{e}] and let
μ0:=σmin​(Σ¯)\mu\_{0}:=\sigma\_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma}) denote its smallest singular value.
If μ0>0\mu\_{0}>0, then there exists r>0r>0 such that for all θ\theta with
‖θ−θ⋆‖≤r\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq r,

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | 𝔼e​[Ω​(θ;e)]≥μ02​‖θ−θ⋆‖.\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]\;\geq\;\tfrac{\mu\_{0}}{2}\,\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert. |  |

In particular, Assumption [4.4](#S4.Thmdefinition4 "Assumption 4.4 (Local order-gap sensitivity). ‣ 4.1 Setting and Assumptions ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") holds locally with constant

###### Proof.

By Jensen and Taylor,
𝔼e​[Ω​(θ⋆+x;e)]≥‖Σ¯​x‖−R​‖x‖2\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)]\geq\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert-R\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}
for some R>0R>0 depending on the second-derivative bounds of QQ and the
integrable dominating function for {Pe}\{P\_{e}\}. Since ‖Σ¯​x‖≥μ0​‖x‖\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert\geq\mu\_{0}\left\lVert x\right\rVert, the right-hand side is at least μ0​‖x‖−R​‖x‖2\mu\_{0}\left\lVert x\right\rVert-R\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}.
Choosing r:=μ0/(2​R)r:=\mu\_{0}/(2R) and restricting to ‖x‖≤r\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq r gives
the result. ∎

In other words, order-gap sensitivity reduces to an invertibility condition on
the *expected commutator Jacobian* Σ¯=𝔼e​[A​Be−Be​A]\bar{\Sigma}=\mathbb{E}\_{e}[AB\_{e}-B\_{e}A].
Verifying this requires only the Jacobians of QQ and PeP\_{e} at a single point,
with no global argument needed. In finite-dimensional settings (or after
choosing a Hilbert coordinate chart on 𝒳\mathcal{X}), the smallest singular value
μ0=σmin​(Σ¯)\mu\_{0}=\sigma\_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma}) is well-defined; on a general Banach
space one replaces the singular-value condition with an equivalent lower bound
on ‖Σ¯​x‖\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert.

###### Remark 4.7 (Second-moment coverage and first-moment sensitivity).

An alternative sufficient condition uses the second-moment matrix of the local
commutator, G:=𝔼e​[Σe⊤​Σe]G:=\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Sigma\_{e}^{\top}\Sigma\_{e}], which we call the
*commutator Gramian*. If

is often easier to verify than the first-moment condition μ0>0\mu\_{0}>0 on
Σ¯\bar{\Sigma}, because first-moment effects can cancel across ee while
second-moment coverage cannot.

From second moment to first moment. If additionally there is a uniform local envelope
Ω​(θ⋆+x;e)≤C​‖x‖\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)\leq C\left\lVert x\right\rVert for all ee (which holds whenever
the Jacobians AA and BeB\_{e} are uniformly bounded), then shrinking the
neighbourhood if necessary, the remainder in the Gramian expansion is
dominated and

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | 𝔼e​[Ω​(θ⋆+x;e)2]≥μ122​‖x‖2.\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)^{2}]\;\geq\;\frac{\mu\_{1}^{2}}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}. |  |

Together with Ω≤C​‖x‖\Omega\leq C\left\lVert x\right\rVert, dividing

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | 𝔼e​[Ω​(θ⋆+x;e)]≥μ122​C​‖x‖.\mathbb{E}\_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)]\;\geq\;\frac{\mu\_{1}^{2}}{2C}\,\left\lVert x\right\rVert. |  |

Thus the order-gap lower bound holds locally with μ=μ12/(2​C)\mu=\mu\_{1}^{2}/(2C). This argument is used
in the RLM verification below.

### 4.3 Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity

Proposition [4.6](#S4.Thmdefinition6 "Proposition 4.6 (Local order-gap sensitivity via Jacobian rank). ‣ 4.2 When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") requires the noiseless hypothesis

### 4.6 The Noise Floor

When σ>0\sigma>0, Theorem [4.10](#S4.Thmdefinition10 "Theorem 4.10 (Contraction of the order-gap). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") gives an *upper bound*
on the asymptotic expected order-gap, which we call the *noise-floor bound*:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | lim supt→∞𝔼[Ω(θt;et)]≤(1+ρ)​σ⏟ε⋆eq+2​γ​ρ​σ1−γ⏟ε⋆traj=:ε⋆.\limsup\_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta\_{t};e\_{t})]\leq\underbrace{(1+\rho)\sigma}\_{\varepsilon\_{\star}^{\text{eq}}}+\underbrace{\frac{2\gamma\rho\sigma}{1-\gamma}}\_{\varepsilon\_{\star}^{\text{traj}}}=:\varepsilon\_{\star}. |  |

The actual asymptotic expected order-gap may be smaller; in special cases it
could be zero despite σ>0\sigma>0. The decomposition nonetheless reveals
which part of the noise floor is reducible.

Fixed-point residual:
ε⋆eq=(1+ρ)​σ\varepsilon\_{\star}^{\text{eq}}=(1+\rho)\sigma is the contribution from
θ⋆\theta^{\star} itself. It is not controlled by trajectory contraction alone:
unless {Pe}\{P\_{e}\} fixes θ⋆\theta^{\star}, the equilibrium residual contributes
to the bound regardless of how well the trajectory contracts.
Trajectory drift term: ε⋆traj=2​γ​ρ​σ/(1−γ)\varepsilon\_{\star}^{\text{traj}}=2\gamma\rho\sigma/(1-\gamma) is reducible: variance-reduced expansion
updates that shrink the effective σ\sigma bring it down.

For the high-probability theorem we use the bounded-noise version of this floor.
Define the *bounded-noise version of the floor*:

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | ε⋆(M):=(1+ρ)​M+2​γ​ρ​M1−γ.\varepsilon\_{\star}^{(M)}:=(1+\rho)M+\frac{2\gamma\rho M}{1-\gamma}. |  | (13) |

This replaces σ\sigma by MM (the a.s. bound on WeW\_{e}) and serves as the
noise-floor quantity for the empirical windowed stopping theorem.

###### Remark 4.15 (Practical implication).

The sufficient stopping theorem requires the threshold ε\varepsilon to exceed
the relevant noise-floor bound: ε⋆\varepsilon\_{\star} in the noiseless expected-gap analysis,
or ε⋆(M)\varepsilon\_{\star}^{(M)} in the high-probability theorem.
When the trajectory drift term dominates, variance reduction
pays dividends. When the fixed-point residual dominates, only modifying PeP\_{e}
itself can help.

### 4.8 Extension to State-Dependent Sampling

The theorems above assume events ete\_{t} are drawn i.i.d. from a fixed
distribution PP, independent of θt\theta\_{t}. For domains where the sampling
kernel depends on the current knowledge state — bandits with adaptive arm
selection, off-policy reinforcement learning with state-dependent behaviour
policies — this independence
fails. We extend the contraction and stopping theorems to the state-dependent
case under uniform conditional moment bounds. The crucial observation is
that the martingale structure underlying the concentration argument
*survives* the move from i.i.d. to state-dependent sampling, so no
mixing-time apparatus is required.

##### Setup.

Let {(θt,et)}t≥0\{(\theta\_{t},e\_{t})\}\_{t\geq 0} be a Markov process on 𝒳×ℰ\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{E}
in which ete\_{t} is drawn from a state-dependent kernel P(⋅∣θt)P(\cdot\mid\theta\_{t}).
Assume the kernel is ℱt\mathcal{F}\_{t}-measurable, i.e., θt\theta\_{t} is
ℱt\mathcal{F}\_{t}-measurable and ete\_{t} is drawn independently of

### 4.9 Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains

The bandit, RLM, and actor-critic cases receive explicit analysis of when
the order-gap reliably tracks convergence below. The SGD case
(Section [5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 Stochastic Gradient Descent ‣ 5 Application Across Five Domains ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) reduces to classical Robbins-Monro stochastic
approximation (Robbins and Monro, [1951](#bib.bib30); Borkar, [2008](#bib.bib7)) when QQ
is trivial; the contraction theorem applies verbatim, and the order-gap
becomes a non-trivial diagnostic in adaptive variants (momentum, Adam,
proximal). The continual-learning case is a qualitative mapping of the
framework rather than a full theorem verification (Section [5](#S5 "5 Application Across Five Domains ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")):
it shows how the QQ/PeP\_{e} decomposition and order-gap control principle
map onto an existing algorithm family.

Effective equilibrium and Jacobian reference point. In stochastic
domains (bandits, RL), the raw sample-level expansion operator satisfies
Pe​(θ⋆)≠θ⋆P\_{e}(\theta^{\star})\neq\theta^{\star} in general: a Gaussian reward draw or
a single TD transition perturbs θ⋆\theta^{\star} stochastically. The noiseless
hypothesis σ=0\sigma=0 of Proposition [4.6](#S4.Thmdefinition6 "Proposition 4.6 (Local order-gap sensitivity via Jacobian rank). ‣ 4.2 When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") therefore does
not hold for the raw operators, and the Jacobian calculations below cannot be
performed at θ⋆\theta^{\star}. The framework’s formal handling of this case is
Proposition [4.9](#S4.Thmdefinition9 "Proposition 4.9 (Noisy local order-gap sensitivity at the effective equilibrium). ‣ 4.3 Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning"): the Jacobians are evaluated at the
effective equilibrium θ∞⋆\theta^{\star}\_{\infty} (the unique fixed point of
T​(θ)=𝔼e​[Q​(Pe​(θ))]T(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\_{e}[Q(P\_{e}(\theta))], established in
Proposition [4.8](#S4.Thmdefinition8 "Proposition 4.8 (Existence of effective equilibrium). ‣ 4.3 Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), and the order-gap sensitivity bound
holds on the annulus where the linear signal in the displacement
θ−θ∞⋆\theta-\theta^{\star}\_{\infty} exceeds the residual noise level
σ∞\sigma^{\infty} at θ∞⋆\theta^{\star}\_{\infty}. The Jacobian calculations below
should be read in this sense: as commutator computations at
θ∞⋆\theta^{\star}\_{\infty} rather than at θ⋆\theta^{\star}. The first-moment or
Gramian conditions, when satisfied, establish the order-gap lower bound on
the relevant annulus around θ∞⋆\theta^{\star}\_{\infty}. The bandit verification
below shows the Gramian path explicitly: in the Bayesian setup with
posterior contraction and mean shrinkage, the first-moment commutator
vanishes at the unbiased equilibrium while the second-moment Gramian remains
non-zero under standard exploration coverage.

#### Bandits: Bayesian State with Posterior Contraction and Mean Shrinkage

We present a Bayesian bandit verification in which the consolidation
operator is genuinely contractive (Assumption [4.1](#S4.Thmdefinition1 "Assumption 4.1 (Contractive consolidation). ‣ 4.1 Setting and Assumptions ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) and the
order-gap sensitivity assumption is verified via the second-moment Gramian
condition of Remark [4.7](#S4.Thmdefinition7 "Remark 4.7 (Second-moment coverage and first-moment sensitivity). ‣ 4.2 When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning"). The verification illustrates
two structural points: (i) the consolidation operator must couple to the
expansion’s evidence-incorporation mechanism (here through variance-mediated
Bayesian updating) to produce a non-trivial commutator; (ii) first-moment
effects can cancel at the unbiased equilibrium while second-moment effects
do not, so the Gramian condition is the natural verification path for
stochastic bandit dynamics.

The verification yields three concrete design criteria:
(i) the consolidation operator must couple to the expansion’s

Expansion.
Pe​(θ)P\_{e}(\theta): a TD update from a transition e=(s,a,r,s′)e=(s,a,r,s^{\prime}), incorporating
new evidence about value (acting on ww).

Consolidation.
Q​(θ)Q(\theta): policy improvement on ψ\psi using advantage estimates from the
critic; see Section [4.9](#S4.SS9 "4.9 Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") for the rigorous formulation
with critic-consistency regularisation.

Order-gap.
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e) measures how much the greedy policy changes when a value
update precedes policy improvement versus when it follows.

Algorithmic consequence.
OpMech suggests setting the actor/critic update ratio adaptively: perform more
critic updates (expansion) when Ω\Omega is large, and more actor updates
(consolidation) when Ω\Omega is small. In Soft Actor-Critic
(Haarnoja et al., [2018](#bib.bib16)), the entropy regularisation and target-network
coefficient jointly govern how aggressively the policy consolidates versus
continues to explore, an implicit order-gap control. The target-network
update rate is discussed further in Section [7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Target Network Update Rate in RL ‣ 7 Hyperparameters as Implicit Order-Gap Control ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning").

### 5.3 Stochastic Gradient Descent

Stochastic gradient descent is the canonical instance of stochastic
approximation (Robbins and Monro, [1951](#bib.bib30); Borkar, [2008](#bib.bib7)), and the
contraction theorem (Theorem [4.10](#S4.Thmdefinition10 "Theorem 4.10 (Contraction of the order-gap). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) reduces to the classical
Robbins-Monro convergence guarantee in this setting. The OpMech framing makes
explicit what is already implicit in adaptive variants of SGD: the per-sample
gradient step (an expansion) and any momentum, averaging, normalization, or
proximal operation that uses only information already in the optimiser state
(a consolidation) play structurally distinct roles.

State.
θ=(w,m,v)\theta=(w,m,v): model parameters together with optimiser state
(first-moment buffer mm, second-moment buffer vv, etc.).

Expansion.
Pe​(θ)P\_{e}(\theta): stochastic gradient step on minibatch ee, updating ww using
the new gradient ∇ℓ​(w,e)\nabla\ell(w,e) and refreshing the optimiser state with
this gradient.

Consolidation.
Q​(θ)Q(\theta): the component of the update that uses only existing optimiser
state, with no new sample. Concrete examples:

- •

  *Vanilla SGD:* Q=idQ=\mathrm{id}. The order-gap is identically
  zero, and OpMech recovers Robbins-Monro stochastic approximation with
  no additional structure.
- •

  *SGD with momentum / Heavy-ball:* QQ advances ww along the
  accumulated momentum buffer mm without consulting a new gradient.
- •

  *Polyak-Ruppert averaging (Polyak and Juditsky, [1992](#bib.bib29)):* QQ
  updates a running average w¯\bar{w} of past iterates.
- •

  *Adam-class methods:* QQ rescales the update direction by the
  second-moment buffer vv, a normalization computed from history rather
  than the new sample.
- •

  *Proximal/regularised SGD:* QQ is the proximal operator
  proxη​r\mathrm{prox}\_{\eta r} for a regulariser rr, applied after the gradient
  step.

Order-gap.
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e) measures the misalignment between the per-sample
gradient direction and the consolidation-induced direction (momentum,
average, normalised, or proximal): how much the algorithm’s effective
descent direction differs depending on whether the gradient is incorporated
before or after the consolidation step. It is zero in vanilla SGD by
construction and non-trivial whenever QQ uses information that the
per-sample step did not.

Relation to Robbins-Monro.
For vanilla SGD the contraction theorem is the Robbins-Monro convergence
theorem; the OpMech framework adds nothing in this case. The contribution
appears in adaptive variants, where the order-gap diagnostic has content
even after Robbins-Monro convergence is established. In particular,
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e) being persistently large signals that the consolidation
direction (momentum / averaged direction / Adam-rescaled direction) is
inconsistent with the per-sample geometry — a regime in which the
optimiser is doing something other than tracking the population gradient,
and in which adaptive step-size or schedule changes are warranted.

Algorithmic consequence.
Learning rate schedules implicitly control Ω\Omega. Warm-up followed by
decay starts with large Ω\Omega and reduces it over time. Cyclical learning
rates (Smith, [2017](#bib.bib34)) periodically reinflate Ω\Omega, re-entering
an expansive phase. Connections to loss-landscape sharpness
(Foret et al., [2021](#bib.bib14)) are immediate: sharp minima exhibit large
order-gap because the per-sample direction varies strongly with ee.

### 5.4 Continual Learning

State.
θ\theta: model parameters, possibly with per-task Fisher information.

Expansion.
Pe​(θ)P\_{e}(\theta): a gradient step on a new task’s loss.

Consolidation.
Q​(θ)Q(\theta): a regularisation step that protects knowledge of previously
learned tasks, e.g., the EWC quadratic penalty (Kirkpatrick et al., [2017](#bib.bib22)) or
PackNet masking (Mallya and Lazebnik, [2018](#bib.bib26)).

Order-gap.
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e) measures the conflict between accommodating new task
evidence and preserving old task knowledge.

Algorithmic consequence.
The regularisation strength λ\lambda in EWC directly controls the order-gap.
A high order-gap indicates task conflict. Depending on whether the system
prioritises plasticity or retention, this can trigger more exploration,
stronger replay, or an adaptive regularisation schedule, not universally a
reduction in λ\lambda, since high conflict can also signal catastrophic
forgetting risk.

### 5.5 Recursive Language Models

State.
θ=S\theta=S: the aggregated state over chunks processed so far.

Expansion.
Pe​(θ)P\_{e}(\theta): process chunk ee, extract structured information and update
the state SS.

Consolidation.
Q​(θ)Q(\theta): the recursive aggregation pass, refine the current state toward
the best current answer without reading new chunks.

Order-gap.
Ω​(θ;e)\Omega(\theta;e) measures how much the consolidated answer shifts when
chunk ee is incorporated before versus after the aggregation pass.

Algorithmic consequence.
Fixed-schedule recursion is implicit order-gap control with a fixed
convergence assumption. OpMech replaces this with measured convergence:
stop recursing when the windowed order-gap falls below ε\varepsilon.
This is developed in Section [6](#S6 "6 Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning").

## 6 Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models

We develop the recursive language model application in detail because it
illustrates two features of the OpMech framework that the other domain
summaries do not: (i) the order-gap as a *convergence criterion* for
iterative inference, replacing heuristic stopping rules; and (ii) the
order-gap as a *control signal for recursion depth*, replacing fixed compute
budgets with an adaptive, evidence-driven signal.

### 6.1 The Problem: Long-Context Reasoning Under Attention Diffusion

Frontier language models exhibit *context rot*: performance degrades as
input length increases, even within the model’s nominal context window
(Zhang et al., [2025](#bib.bib43)). Recursive language models address this by treating the
long context as an external environment rather than fitting it into a single
forward pass (Zhang et al., [2025](#bib.bib43)), introducing a control problem: when should
the model stop recursing? When should it consolidate partial answers?

### 6.2 Operator Identification

Consider a recursive language model processing a long document decomposed into
chunks e1,e2,…,ene\_{1},e\_{2},\ldots,e\_{n}. The model maintains an aggregated state

answers not previously in SS.

Q​(S)=Aggregate​(S)Q(S)=\mathrm{Aggregate}(S): resolve contradictions among extracted claims,
compress redundant information, and sharpen the answer distribution toward the

order-gap is:

### 6.4 Independent Evidence: Two Systems Converge on the Q/P Decomposition

The QQ/PeP\_{e} decomposition in recursive language models is not an
interpretive framework imposed post hoc. Recent work provides independent
corroborating evidence.

The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) (Jolicoeur-Martineau, [2025](#bib.bib19)), described
in a paper titled *Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks*,
is a 7M-parameter recursive reasoning model achieving 45% on ARC-AGI-1.
TRM provides corroborating evidence: its recursive loop separates
latent-state refinement (zz-update, which recomputes the reasoning state
given fixed input) from answer commitment (yy-update, which commits current
reasoning to an answer without new external input). This distinction is
analogous to the expansion/consolidation split, though TRM does not process
external document chunks in the same way as RLM. TRM’s ablations show that
this separation is essential: neither operation alone suffices.

Recursive language models (Zhang et al., [2025](#bib.bib43)) exhibit the same structure
from the opposite end of the scale, emerging from the engineering constraint
that long contexts cannot fit in a single forward pass.

That two independent systems converge on the same QQ/PeP\_{e} structure is
evidence that the decomposition is structural. OpMech provides the
mathematical language to recognise this and, through the order-gap, to improve
upon the heuristic control mechanisms both systems currently use.

### 6.5 Predictions and Empirical Signatures

The OpMech framework, applied to recursive language models, generates several
testable predictions. We state them here as part of the framework’s
empirical content; experimental validation, including measurement of the
Ω\Omega trace and ablations against fixed-budget recursion, is reported in
a companion paper.

1. 1.

   Monotone Ω\Omega decay. On tasks where the model converges to
   a correct answer, Ω​(St;et)\Omega(S\_{t};e\_{t}) should decrease approximately
   geometrically across chunks after an initial transient. The empirical
   signature is an order-gap trace that is approximately log-linear in
   chunk count.
2. 2.

   Ω\Omega-driven stopping matches or outperforms fixed-schedule
   stopping. Equal or better task performance at equal or lower compute.
3. 3.

   Ω\Omega-driven stopping matches learned halting. The order-gap
   criterion requires no supervised convergence labels; if it matches a
   supervised classifier, it demonstrates that OpMech extracts competitive
   convergence information.
4. 4.

   Consolidation-trigger depth outperforms uniform depth.
   Using ([25](#S6.E25 "In Recursion-depth control (consolidation scheduling). ‣ 6.3 Three Control Applications ‣ 6 Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) to trigger consolidation should produce
   more efficient recursion than strategies that consolidate uniformly after every chunk.

## 7 Hyperparameters as Implicit Order-Gap Control

Many familiar hyperparameters across domains can be reinterpreted as implicit
mechanisms for controlling the order-gap.

### 7.1 Exploration Rates in Bandits

In ε\varepsilon-greedy, the exploration rate εt\varepsilon\_{t} directly controls
the probability of selecting a non-greedy action. A decaying schedule
εt∝1/t\varepsilon\_{t}\propto 1/t assumes the order-gap decays at rate O​(1/t)O(1/t).
When the assumption is wrong (non-stationary environment), the schedule fails.
OpMech replaces the assumed decay with the *measured* order-gap,
yielding εt=ϕ​(Ω¯t)\varepsilon\_{t}=\phi(\bar{\Omega}\_{t}).

### 7.2 Target Network Update Rate in RL

In DQN (Mnih et al., [2015](#bib.bib27)) and its successors, target-network updating
stabilises the consolidation of the value function. For *hard
updates* with period CC (copy online weights every CC steps), larger CC
means less frequent consolidation and a larger order-gap; smaller CC means
more frequent consolidation and a smaller order-gap. For *soft updates*
with coefficient τ\tau (θ−←τ​θ+(1−τ)​θ−\theta^{-}\leftarrow\tau\theta+(1-\tau)\theta^{-}),
larger τ\tau means stronger tracking of the online network, i.e. more
frequent effective consolidation. OpMech suggests adapting the update
frequency or coefficient based on Ω\Omega: consolidate more when the
order-gap is small, and allow the online network to expand freely when
Ω\Omega is large.

### 7.3 Learning Rate Schedules in SGD

Warm-up followed by decay inflates then contracts the expansion operator,
yielding a large then small order-gap. Cyclical learning rates
(Smith, [2017](#bib.bib34)) periodically reinflate the order-gap, interpretable
as the optimiser re-entering an expansive phase.

### 7.4 Regularisation Strength in Continual Learning

In EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., [2017](#bib.bib22)), large λ\lambda suppresses the order-gap
by making QQ dominant. The adaptive rule, modulate λ\lambda in response to
Ω\Omega, provides a principled alternative to fixing λ\lambda by
cross-validation.

### 7.5 Recursion Depth in Recursive Language Models

Fixed recursion depth and compute budgets are implicit order-gap controls with
fixed convergence assumptions. The Ω\Omega-based stopping criterion
([24](#S6.E24 "In Convergence criterion (stopping). ‣ 6.3 Three Control Applications ‣ 6 Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) and recursion trigger ([25](#S6.E25 "In Recursion-depth control (consolidation scheduling). ‣ 6.3 Three Control Applications ‣ 6 Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")) replace
these with measured convergence, allocating compute where the evidence demands
it.

## 8 Related Work

Explore-exploit tradeoffs.
The exploration-exploitation dilemma is foundational in bandits
(Lai and Robbins, [1985](#bib.bib24); Lattimore and Szepesvári, [2020](#bib.bib25)), RL (Sutton and Barto, [2018](#bib.bib36)), and
Bayesian optimization (Srinivas et al., [2010](#bib.bib35)). Classical bandit algorithms
include UCB-style upper-confidence methods (Kaufmann et al., [2012](#bib.bib20)) and
Thompson sampling with its modern regret analysis
(Russo and Van Roy, [2014](#bib.bib31)). OpMech differs from these by proposing a
*domain-independent observable*, the order-gap, that measures the
dilemma’s severity and can drive algorithmic decisions; rather than
specifying an exploration rule, it specifies a quantity that any
exploration rule should track.

Stochastic approximation.
The Robbins-Monro framework
(Robbins and Monro, [1951](#bib.bib30); Borkar, [2008](#bib.bib7)) for iterating a contractive
operator under stochastic perturbations is the classical setting for the
contraction theorem (Theorem [4.10](#S4.Thmdefinition10 "Theorem 4.10 (Contraction of the order-gap). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")); the proof is essentially
a stochastic approximation argument applied to the pathwise contraction
inequality ([10](#S4.E10 "In Proof. ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")). Polyak-Juditsky averaging
(Polyak and Juditsky, [1992](#bib.bib29)) achieves optimal asymptotic rates in this regime.
The contractive-in-expectation regime of
Remark [4.12](#S4.Thmdefinition12 "Remark 4.12 (Polynomial rates under weaker contraction). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") — yielding O​(1/t)O(1/\sqrt{t}) rather than
geometric rates — is the standard regime of stochastic approximation theory.
OpMech’s contribution beyond this literature is the order-gap as a
domain-independent observable: stochastic approximation provides rates of
convergence; OpMech provides a real-time diagnostic for whether convergence
has occurred, computable from the trajectory itself.

Expectation-Maximization and alternating optimization.
The EM algorithm (Dempster et al., [1977](#bib.bib12)) is the canonical two-operator
alternating framework: the E-step is the natural PeP\_{e} (incorporating
evidence into sufficient statistics), the M-step the natural QQ (parameter
consolidation given fixed statistics). EM’s monotone likelihood
improvement (Wu, [1983](#bib.bib39)) is a Lyapunov argument paralleling
the contraction theorem (Theorem [4.10](#S4.Thmdefinition10 "Theorem 4.10 (Contraction of the order-gap). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), and the
finite-sample analysis of Balakrishnan et al. ([2017](#bib.bib3)) parallels
Theorem [4.17](#S4.Thmdefinition17 "Theorem 4.17 (Empirical windowed stopping, 𝜎>0). ‣ The noisy regime: 𝜎>0 with bounded noise ‣ 4.7 Stopping Guarantees ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") in spirit, characterizing the trajectory’s
distance to a population fixed point under bounded sample noise. The
order-gap question — does the ordering of the two operators matter at
convergence? — is exactly the EM convergence diagnostic, but EM monitors
the likelihood, which is often expensive or intractable to compute in
latent-variable models. The order-gap operates directly on operator outputs,
providing the same diagnostic in a domain-independent form applicable
wherever EM-style alternation occurs and beyond.

Non-commutativity in learning.
The idea that the order of learning operations matters has appeared in
curriculum learning (Bengio et al., [2009](#bib.bib9)), experience replay
(Schaul et al., [2016](#bib.bib33)), and meta-learning (Finn et al., [2017](#bib.bib13)), where
the inner-loop (PeP\_{e}) and outer-loop (QQ) distinction is explicit.

Operator-theoretic approaches.
Kernel methods (Schölkopf and Smola, [2002](#bib.bib32)), Koopman operator theory
(Brunton and Kutz, [2022](#bib.bib10)), and NTK analysis (Jacot et al., [2018](#bib.bib18)) all use
operator-theoretic tools. OpMech is distinct in studying the
*interaction* between two operators rather than the spectral properties
of a single one.

Alternating projections and ADMM.
Order-dependent operator compositions have been studied extensively in convex
optimization. Von Neumann’s alternating projection theorem
(see Bauschke and Borwein, [1996](#bib.bib4), for a modern treatment), Bregman
alternating algorithms, and the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers
(Boyd et al., [2011](#bib.bib8)) all use sequenced applications of contractive operators
where the order of application affects convergence behaviour. ADMM’s
primal/dual residuals serve as the standard convergence diagnostic in this
literature. OpMech generalizes the alternating-operator structure beyond
convex optimization to general adaptive learning, and the order-gap subsumes
ADMM’s residual diagnostics in the convex case while extending to settings
(bandits, RL, recursive language models) where no natural primal/dual
structure exists.

Two-timescale stochastic approximation and adaptive sampling.
The OpMech dynamics θt+1=Q∘Pet​(θt)\theta\_{t+1}=Q\circ P\_{e\_{t}}(\theta\_{t}) couple a
contractive map QQ with a stochastic update PetP\_{e\_{t}}, and the

Fixed-point iteration and equilibrium models.
The OpMech dynamics admit a unique fixed point θ⋆\theta^{\star} of the
consolidation operator QQ, and the framework’s central object — the
order-gap — measures how far iteration has progressed toward that fixed
point. Deep equilibrium models (Bai et al., [2019](#bib.bib2)) similarly
parameterize neural-network layers as fixed points of contractive maps and
use implicit differentiation to train them. The two frameworks address
adjacent problems: DEQs ask how to compute and differentiate fixed points;
OpMech asks how to detect that an adaptive system has reached one. The
operator-theoretic abstraction is shared.

Adaptive hyperparameters.
Adam (Kingma and Ba, [2015](#bib.bib21)), LARS (You et al., [2017](#bib.bib42)), and Bayesian UCB
(Kaufmann et al., [2012](#bib.bib20)) adjust algorithm parameters based on observed
quantities. OpMech contributes a *unified* observable, the order-gap, as
the basis for adaptive control across all of these settings.

Recursive and iterative inference.
Recursive language models (Zhang et al., [2025](#bib.bib43)) scale LLM context by recursive
decomposition; TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, [2025](#bib.bib19)) achieves strong
reasoning with iterative refinement; tree-of-thoughts
(Yao et al., [2023](#bib.bib40)) and self-consistency (Wang et al., [2023](#bib.bib41))
sample multiple reasoning trajectories and aggregate them. PonderNet
(Banino et al., [2021](#bib.bib1)) and Universal Transformers
(Dehghani et al., [2019](#bib.bib11)) use learned halting. OpMech provides a
theoretically grounded alternative across these settings: stop when the
order-gap indicates convergence, without requiring supervised halting
labels or hand-designed aggregation rules. For tree-of-thoughts and
self-consistency in particular, the order-gap supplies a principled
criterion for when an additional reasoning trace would change the
aggregated answer — and when it would not.

## 9 Discussion

What OpMech is and is not.
OpMech identifies a measurable quantity, the order-gap, present in every
adaptive system where consolidation and expansion interact, and proposes using
that quantity as a domain-independent control signal. It does not claim that
all learning algorithms are the same: the operators QQ and PeP\_{e} look very
different across domains, and the state spaces are different. The unification
is at the level of dynamical structure and algorithmic principle, not domain
specifics.

Corroborating evidence and contribution to algorithm design.
The independent convergence of TRM and RLM on the QQ/PeP\_{e} decomposition,
from opposite ends of the model-scale spectrum, supports the view that the
decomposition reflects a recurring mechanism rather than an interpretive
choice. The RL derivation yields a concrete consequence of this: canonical
actor-critic consolidation is insensitive to policy directions, and the
order-gap cannot detect policy errors unless consolidation is augmented with
a policy-critic consistency term. The order-gap sensitivity constant scales
linearly in β′\beta^{\prime}, making the restoration explicit and quantitative. A
parallel finding for RLMs (order-gap sensitivity as a coverage
condition) prescribes coverage-weighted over greedy chunk selection.

Limitations.
Four limitations should be noted. First, the SGD application is the weakest:
identifying momentum with expansion is approximate, and the order-gap
interpretation remains qualitative there. Second, the recursive intelligence
predictions are stated as hypotheses in this paper; experimental validation
is reported in a companion paper. Third, the noisy-regime stopping bounds
use Azuma–Hoeffding concentration on the martingale residuals
Ω​(θs;es)−gs\Omega(\theta\_{s};e\_{s})-g\_{s}; sharper constants are likely available via
Bernstein-type or self-normalised martingale concentration
(Howard et al., [2021](#bib.bib17)), particularly when the conditional variance
Vare​[Ω​(θs;e)]\mathrm{Var}\_{e}[\Omega(\theta\_{s};e)] admits a useful upper bound, and this
is a natural direction for follow-up. Fourth, the bandit verification in
Section [4.9](#S4.SS9 "4.9 Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") establishes joint sensitivity on the
(μ^,σ^2)(\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma}^{2}) state via the Gramian condition on the variance
subspace (μvar>0\mu\_{\mathrm{var}}>0) and the first-moment commutator on the
mean subspace (μmean=O​(λ)\mu\_{\mathrm{mean}}=O(\lambda)). The joint constant
μ=min⁡(μmean,μvar)\mu=\min(\mu\_{\mathrm{mean}},\mu\_{\mathrm{var}}) is positive whenever
the mean-shrinkage rate λ>0\lambda>0 is fixed. In the unbiased limit
λ→0\lambda\to 0 the mean-block sensitivity vanishes; this is structural
rather than a defect of the verification, reflecting that an unbiased Bayes
update has no first-moment commutator with itself.

The research programme.
This paper establishes the framework and develops the recursive intelligence
application. The primary near-term directions are: full order-gap sensitivity
verification for bandits with a correct consolidation operator, including
regret analysis across stationary, non-stationary, and structured settings;
controlled experiments on Ω\Omega-driven stopping and depth control for
recursive language models; and adaptive actor-critic RL using the
policy-critic consistency term, testing whether restoring full rank produces
measurable convergence improvements. For SGD with non-trivial QQ (momentum,
Adam, proximal variants), empirical validation of order-gap-based learning
rate schedules and warm-up strategies; for continual learning, similar
validation of order-gap-based regularisation strength schedules.

## 10 Conclusion

We have presented Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics, a framework that
formalises the consolidation-expansion tension in adaptive learning as a
stochastic dynamical system driven by two non-commuting operators QQ and
PeP\_{e}. The order-gap, the distance between the two orderings of these
operators, is a domain-independent observable that tracks whether a learning
system has settled.

The formal guarantees are: geometric decay of the expected
order-gap (Theorem [4.10](#S4.Thmdefinition10 "Theorem 4.10 (Contraction of the order-gap). ‣ 4.4 Contraction of the Order-Gap ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), a lower bound making
the order-gap a proxy for suboptimality (Proposition [4.14](#S4.Thmdefinition14 "Proposition 4.14 (Order-gap lower bounds suboptimality). ‣ 4.5 Operational Lower Bound ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")),
and stopping guarantees in both noiseless and bounded-noise regimes
(Theorems [4.16](#S4.Thmdefinition16 "Theorem 4.16 (Expected-gap stopping, 𝜎=0). ‣ The noiseless regime: 𝜎=0 ‣ 4.7 Stopping Guarantees ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")–[4.17](#S4.Thmdefinition17 "Theorem 4.17 (Empirical windowed stopping, 𝜎>0). ‣ The noisy regime: 𝜎>0 with bounded noise ‣ 4.7 Stopping Guarantees ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), with a formal
restatement of the noisy-regime stopping bounds at the effective equilibrium
θ∞⋆\theta^{\star}\_{\infty} (Corollary [4.20](#S4.Thmdefinition20 "Corollary 4.20 (Stopping at the effective equilibrium). ‣ The noisy regime: 𝜎>0 with bounded noise ‣ 4.7 Stopping Guarantees ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")). The
state-dependent sampling case is handled by replacing marginal expectation
bounds with uniform conditional bounds; the i.i.d. theorems then carry over
verbatim (Theorems [4.22](#S4.Thmdefinition22 "Theorem 4.22 (Contraction under state-dependent sampling). ‣ Setup. ‣ 4.8 Extension to State-Dependent Sampling ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")–[4.23](#S4.Thmdefinition23 "Theorem 4.23 (Empirical stopping under state-dependent sampling). ‣ Setup. ‣ 4.8 Extension to State-Dependent Sampling ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")),
because martingale concentration on conditional-mean residuals survives the
move from i.i.d. to state-dependent sampling without any mixing-time
correction. The order-gap sensitivity condition reduces to a Jacobian-level
rank condition (Proposition [4.6](#S4.Thmdefinition6 "Proposition 4.6 (Local order-gap sensitivity via Jacobian rank). ‣ 4.2 When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning"), with the noisy-regime
extension Proposition [4.9](#S4.Thmdefinition9 "Proposition 4.9 (Noisy local order-gap sensitivity at the effective equilibrium). ‣ 4.3 Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning") centred at the effective
equilibrium of Proposition [4.8](#S4.Thmdefinition8 "Proposition 4.8 (Existence of effective equilibrium). ‣ 4.3 Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), analysed in three
domains: for bandits, a Bayesian setup with posterior contraction and mean
shrinkage gives a contractive consolidation operator and a non-trivial
commutator structure verified through the second-moment Gramian condition
under standard exploration coverage; for RLMs, the condition becomes a
second-moment coverage condition; and for linear actor-critic RL, it reduces
to a rank condition on the locally identifiable actor-critic directions
(Theorems [4.24](#S4.Thmdefinition24 "Theorem 4.24 (Baseline actor-critic is insensitive to policy directions). ‣ Rank deficiency of baseline. ‣ Reinforcement Learning: Linear Actor-Critic ‣ 4.9 Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")–[4.25](#S4.Thmdefinition25 "Theorem 4.25 (Order-gap sensitivity for regularised actor-critic). ‣ Identifiable subspace and order-gap sensitivity conditions. ‣ Reinforcement Learning: Linear Actor-Critic ‣ 4.9 Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains ‣ 4 Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees ‣ Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning")), with an
explicit rank-deficiency diagnosis and the augmentation needed to fix it.
The RL derivation prescribes a policy-critic consistency term in consolidation,
with the order-gap sensitivity constant scaling linearly in β′\beta^{\prime}.

The same QQ/PeP\_{e} structure that governs bandit exploration also governs when
a recursive language model should stop reading, consolidate its answer, or
recurse deeper. That two independent engineering systems (TRM and RLM, from
opposite ends of the model-scale spectrum) converge on this decomposition
suggests it captures something real about how adaptive reasoning works.
